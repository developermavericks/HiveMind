How this works

1 · Poll and cluster

For each of 15 Indian publications the app asks Google News for everything it ran in the last 24 hours (site:<domain> when:24h), adding narrower recent windows when Google's ~100-item ceiling is hit. Headlines about the same event are merged into one story with an outlet count.

2 · Read each story, with no company in mind

The app resolves the article text (falling back to the headline) and the LLM records what happened, who and what is affected (area, direction, severity, horizon) and where. It is told to ignore any client or industry's interests.

3 · File every impact under a keyword bracket — never repeated

Each impact is filed under a keyword bracket: a broad one- or two-word area such as People, Education, Economy, Finance, AI & Tech, Startups, Climate, Energy, Health, Politics. There is a fixed starter list (22) and each bracket has keywords describing it. The LLM sees the whole list for every story and must pick the closest; it may add a new bracket only when an impact is clearly outside all of them, and a proposed new bracket is merged into an existing one if its name or keywords overlap (≥60%). The starter brackets are never merged away, so the same area is never created twice.

4 · Density

A story's weight in a bracket is severity × coverage × basis: severity is its strongest impact in that bracket, coverage is 1 + log₂(outlets), basis is 1 for full text and 0.7 for headline-only. Weights go into 3-hour bins by publication time and are smoothed.

5 · Company lens

Upload a company's sectors. For each bracket the LLM is shown the area's most significant current stories and judges, using only the sector names and descriptions you gave, which sectors it matters to — relevance 0–1, direct or indirect, the front it hits (demand, cost, supply chain, regulation…), and an article angle for that exact sector × area pairing. Judgements are cached and refreshed when an area's stories have grown by about half or aged, so angles track the news. Sector exposure = Σ bracket density × relevance. Each recommendation's rank = bracket weight × relevance.

Read with care

Relevance, severity and direct/indirect are model judgement, not measurement, and have not been checked against human-labelled examples. The weighting constants are reasoned defaults. More outlets doesn't always mean more important. Sector descriptions you provide matter: richer descriptions give better matches. Treat the output as a ranked list of topics to investigate.
